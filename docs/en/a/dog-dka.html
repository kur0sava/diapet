Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA) in Dogs

Complications · 5 min

What it is in simple terms

When there's too little insulin, cells can't take glucose from the blood and "starve" even though blood sugar is high. So the body starts breaking down fat for energy. The byproduct is ketones. When too many build up, the blood turns acidic. That's ketoacidosis — a life-threatening state.

Warning signs

  • Vomiting — especially repeated
  • Complete refusal to eat for more than 12 hours
  • Severe lethargy — the dog lies down, barely responds
  • Acetone breath — a sweet smell, like nail polish remover
  • Rapid, labored breathing
  • Dehydration — dry gums, scruff skin slowly returns to place

If you notice two or more signs — act immediately.

When to rush to the clinic IMMEDIATELY

Don't wait until morning, don't hope it passes. DKA is an emergency. Bring your dog if:

  • Vomiting + refusal to eat
  • Acetone smell + lethargy
  • The dog can't stand or is wobbling
  • Loss of consciousness or seizures

Every hour counts. The sooner treatment starts, the better the outcome.

What happens at the clinic

  1. IV fluids — restoring hydration and electrolytes
  2. IV short-acting insulin — under constant monitoring
  3. Potassium and phosphorus correction — both drop during DKA
  4. Monitoring — glucose, electrolytes, ketones every 2–4 hours
  5. Finding the trigger — DKA is often driven by infection, pancreatitis, or a concurrent illness

Treatment usually takes 2–5 days in hospital.

Why DKA happens in dogs

  • Diabetes not yet diagnosed (DKA is often the first sign)
  • Missed insulin injections
  • Dose became insufficient
  • Infection — a very common trigger in dogs (especially urinary tract)
  • Pancreatitis — a frequent companion of canine diabetes
  • Diestrus or heat in an unspayed female
  • Hyperadrenocorticism (Cushing's disease)

Home ketone testing

Urine test strips (Ketostix and similar) are a simple home check:

  1. Collect urine in a clean container
  2. Dip the strip, read after 15 seconds
  3. Trace (+/−): increase monitoring
  4. "+" + feeling unwell: vet today
  5. "++"/"+++": emergency clinic

Important nuance: strips detect acetoacetate but not β-hydroxybutyrate (the main ketone in DKA). A negative result does not rule out DKA. If your dog is unwell — go to the clinic regardless of the strip.

How to prevent it

  • Never skip injections — if unsure the dose went in, skip rather than inject twice
  • Watch appetite — refusal to eat for over a day = vet visit
  • Spay your female — heat cycles destabilize diabetes and raise DKA risk
  • Keep test strips — check urine if glucose stays above 14–15 mmol/L
  • Treat infections promptly — hidden UTIs are a common cause

Most importantly — don't panic

With timely treatment, most dogs recover from DKA. Yes, it's serious and frightening. But you can handle this, and so can your dog.

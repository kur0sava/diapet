Diabetic Neuropathy in Cats

If you've noticed your cat walking strangely — sort of "sinking down" on their hind legs — this could be diabetic neuropathy. It looks alarming, but we have good news: in cats, this condition is reversible!

Neuropathy happens when prolonged high blood sugar damages the nerves, especially in the hind legs.

What Does It Look Like?

A healthy cat walks on its toes — like a little ballerina. With neuropathy, your cat starts walking flat on its feet, almost like a person. It looks as if the hind legs are "buckling."

This is called a plantigrade stance, and it's one of the most visible signs of diabetes.

Other Signs

  • Weakness in the hind legs
  • Wobbly, unsteady walk
  • Your cat can't jump onto their favorite couch
  • Decreased sensation in the paws
  • Sometimes the cat avoids moving because of discomfort

If you notice any of this — it's definitely a reason to see the vet, but not a reason to panic.

Why Does This Happen?

When blood sugar stays high for a long time, a substance called sorbitol builds up in the nerve cells. It damages the protective coating around nerve fibers, and nerve signals start traveling more slowly.

In simple terms: the nerves in the paws "slow down," and it becomes harder for your cat to control them.

Treatment and Prognosis

Here's the important part: neuropathy in cats is reversible! When blood sugar returns to normal, the nerves gradually heal.

Recovery typically takes 1 to 6 months with good blood sugar control. Patience is your friend here.

How you can help:

  • The most important thing — get blood sugar under control
  • Vitamin B12 (methylcobalamin) — 0.5-1 mg orally, once daily (mix with food); supports nerve recovery. Do NOT inject — the oral form is effective and safer
  • Gentle paw massage
  • Soft, comfortable bedding
  • Food bowls and litter box with low sides — to make life easier for your kitty

When to Seek Urgent Help

Call your vet immediately if:

  • The condition suddenly worsens
  • The hind legs become completely paralyzed
  • You see signs of pain

Remember: your attention and care are already half the treatment.

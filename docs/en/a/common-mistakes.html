Common Mistakes in Diabetes Management

Nobody is born an expert in feline diabetes. We all learn as we go, and mistakes are a normal part of the journey. Don't beat yourself up if you recognize something from this list. The important thing is — you're here and reading, which means you care deeply about your cat.

Mistake 1: Injecting Without Food

The danger: if your cat hasn't eaten but gets insulin, blood sugar can drop dangerously low. This is called hypoglycemia, and it's serious.

Simple rule: food first, injection second. Always.

If your cat refuses to eat:

  • Try a different food
  • Wait 30 minutes
  • Still not eating? Skip the dose and call your vet. Skipping a dose is safer than injecting a hungry cat.

Mistake 2: Injections "Whenever It Works Out"

The danger: when the time between injections varies, blood sugar bounces up and down. This undermines stability.

Simple rule: every 12 hours, with a maximum 30-minute deviation. Set an alarm — it genuinely helps!

Mistake 3: Adjusting the Dose Yourself

The danger: you see a high reading and want to add more insulin — that's a completely understandable impulse. But without the full picture (a glucose curve), you could do harm.

Simple rule: any dose change happens only after talking to your vet. They see the complete picture.

Mistake 4: Feeding Dry Food

The danger: dry food is 30-50% carbohydrates. For a diabetic cat, that's like giving candy for breakfast to a person with diabetes.

Simple rule: wet food with low carbohydrates only. You'll notice the difference!

Mistake 5: Missing Signs of Low Blood Sugar

Hypoglycemia is when blood sugar drops too low. Here's what to watch for:

  • Weakness, staggering
  • Trembling
  • Your cat seems "lost," disoriented
  • Seizures

What to do right now: if the cat is conscious and can swallow — smear honey or glucose syrup onto the gums (don't pour anything into the mouth!). If unconscious or seizing — do NOT give anything by mouth (aspiration risk), just apply honey to the gums (absorbs through the mucosa) and get to a clinic immediately. Keep honey on hand at all times — it could save a life.

Mistake 6: Improper Insulin Storage

Insulin is a fussy medication:

  • Opened vial: up to 28 days (for Glargine) or per instructions
  • Keep unopened stock in the fridge at 2-8°C, ideally on a middle shelf rather than in the door
  • Never freeze!
  • If the insulin looks cloudy — throw it out, it's spoiled

Mistake 7: Refusing to Monitor at Home

We understand — pricking your cat's ear feels scary. But here's why it matters: at the clinic, stress distorts blood sugar, and the vet sees an inaccurate picture.

Home monitoring gives you:

  • Real numbers without stress
  • Early detection of dangerous blood sugar drops
  • Accurate data for dose adjustment

Ask your vet to show you the technique — it's easier than it sounds. Most cats get used to it quickly.

Mistake 8: Not Reducing Dose When Remission Approaches

The danger: your cat is heading toward remission — insulin needs are dropping, but the dose stays the same. Result: severe hypoglycemia.

The alarm signal: nadir below 4-5 mmol/L at a stable dose, especially if it repeats. This isn't "excellent control" — it's a signal to reduce the dose!

Mistake 9: Not Measuring the Nadir

The danger: many owners only check pre-injection glucose. But without knowing the nadir (lowest point), it's easy to miss dangerous hypoglycemia or the Somogyi effect.

Simple rule: full glucose curve every 1-2 weeks during dose adjustment, every 1-3 months when stable.

Mistake 10: Mistaking Stress for Poor Control

The danger: you brought your cat to the clinic, glucose read 22 mmol/L. Panic! You increase the dose → hypoglycemia at home.

Simple rule: never adjust the dose based on a single clinic reading. In cats, stress can spike blood sugar by 10-15 mmol/L within minutes. Trust home readings and fructosamine.

Golden Rules — Remember These Five

  1. Food first, injection second — never the other way around
  2. Consistency is the foundation of stability
  3. Low-carbohydrate diet
  4. Measure blood sugar at home — including the nadir!
  5. Vet visits every 3-6 months when things are stable

You're doing great. Really.

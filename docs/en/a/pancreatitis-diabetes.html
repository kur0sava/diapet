Pancreatitis and Diabetes in Cats

Why These Two Conditions Are Connected

The pancreas performs two critical jobs: producing digestive enzymes and making insulin. When it becomes inflamed (pancreatitis), both functions suffer.

Research shows that up to 50-80% of diabetic cats have signs of chronic pancreatitis. This is not your fault — it's simply how the body works.

How Pancreatitis Affects Diabetes

When the pancreas is inflamed, it doesn't work as well:

  • Glucose levels "jump around" — good days alternate with high readings
  • The usual insulin dose stops working
  • Remission becomes harder to achieve — inflammation prevents beta cells from recovering
  • Your cat eats poorly — and without food, giving insulin isn't safe

It feels like a vicious cycle, but it can be broken.

Signs of a Pancreatitis Flare-Up

In cats, pancreatitis often presents "quietly":

  • Decreased appetite or complete refusal to eat — the most common sign
  • Lethargy — hiding away, sleeping more than usual
  • Vomiting — can be a single episode or repeated
  • Abdominal pain — won't let you touch their belly, hunches their back
  • Diarrhea or changes in stool

The tricky part: symptoms can be very subtle. Sometimes the only sign is "the cat just seems off." Trust your instincts — you know your cat better than anyone.

When to See the Vet

  • Your cat hasn't eaten for more than 24 hours
  • Vomiting 2 or more times
  • Glucose consistently above 20 mmol/L on the usual dose
  • Your cat looks painful or depressed
  • Dehydration (dry gums, skin that tents)

Don't wait — with pancreatitis, timing matters.

Feeding During a Flare-Up

During the first days:

  • Small portions — 6-8 tiny meals are better than 2 large ones
  • Moderate-fat, easily digestible food — in cats (unlike dogs) strict fat restriction is not well supported and can worsen weight loss and the risk of hepatic lipidosis in a cat that stops eating. Reserve strict fat limits for the acute phase only, under veterinary guidance (Zoran 2009, Xenoulis & Steiner 2015)
  • Warm food — gently warm it to improve appetite
  • Smooth texture — pate works better than chunks

If your cat won't eat at all:

  • Don't give insulin without food!
  • Try plain meat baby food (without onion or garlic)
  • If refusal lasts more than 24 hours — see the vet

Long-Term Management

  • Consistent feeding schedule — same times, same portions
  • High-quality wet food — low-carb AND moderate-fat
  • Minimize stress — stress triggers flare-ups
  • Regular blood work — pancreatic lipase (fPLI) every 3-6 months
  • Vitamin B12 — chronic pancreatitis often causes deficiency
  • Watch their weight — neither obesity nor wasting is acceptable

You're Doing Everything Right

Managing two chronic conditions at once is truly heroic. There will be good days and tough days. But many cats with pancreatitis and diabetes live long, happy lives. Your care and love are exactly what your furry friend needs.

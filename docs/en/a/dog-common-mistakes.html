Common Mistakes in Managing a Dog with Diabetes

Tips · 7 min

Nobody is born an expert. Mistakes are a normal part of the journey. Don't beat yourself up if you recognize something here — you're already here and learning.

Mistake 1: Injecting Without Food

Danger: if the dog hasn't eaten but gets insulin, blood sugar can drop too low (hypoglycemia).

Rule: food first, injection second. Always. Didn't eat half the portion — don't give the full dose, contact your vet.

Mistake 2: Injections "Whenever It Works Out"

Danger: drifting intervals rock blood sugar up and down.

Rule: every 12 hours, max 30-60 minute deviation. Set an alarm.

Mistake 3: Adjusting the Dose Yourself

Danger: a single high reading is scary and tempts you to add insulin — but without a glucose curve it's easy to do harm (including triggering the Somogyi effect).

Rule: any dose change happens only after talking to your vet.

Mistake 4: Feeding Feline Diabetic Food

Danger: cats need low-carb high-protein food, but a dog needs high fiber and moderate fat. Cat food is unsuitable for dogs and raises pancreatitis risk.

Rule: use a canine diabetic diet only (Royal Canin Diabetic Canine, Hill's w/d, etc.).

Mistake 5: Inconsistent Walks

Danger: a sudden active walk sharply drops blood sugar — a hypoglycemia risk. This is a dog difference: exercise affects their glucose more than a cat's.

Rule: the same amount of exercise every day. Planning a long hike — discuss an adjustment with your vet.

Mistake 6: Missing Signs of Low Blood Sugar

Weakness, trembling, staggering, disorientation, seizures. Keep honey on hand.

What to do: if the dog is conscious and can swallow — honey/syrup on the gums, then food. Unconscious or seizing — do NOT put anything in the mouth, a thin layer of honey on the gums and rush to the clinic.

Mistake 7: Not Spaying a Female

Danger: in an unspayed female, progesterone causes insulin resistance; every heat destabilizes diabetes and raises DKA risk.

Rule: spaying is part of diabetes treatment in females. Discuss it with your vet.

Mistake 8: Improper Insulin Storage

  • Opened vial: shelf life depends on the product (Caninsulin/Vetsulin ~42 days, NPH/glargine ~28 days)
  • Store in the fridge (per label), never freeze
  • Roll suspensions (Caninsulin, NPH), don't shake
  • Cloudy, discolored, or flaky — discard it

Mistake 9: Ignoring Cataracts

Danger: up to 75-80% of dogs go blind from diabetic cataracts within a year. Many owners assume "if she's blind, nothing can be done."

Rule: canine cataracts are operable — with timely referral, sight can be restored. Talk to an ophthalmologist.

Mistake 10: Confusing Illness-Driven High Sugar with Poor Control

Danger: blood sugar suddenly "won't obey" the dose. The cause may be a hidden urinary tract infection, pancreatitis, or Cushing's — not "too little insulin."

Rule: a sudden loss of control = look for a concurrent illness with your vet, don't blindly increase the dose.

Golden Rules

  1. Food first, injection second — never the reverse
  2. Consistent injections and walks
  3. Canine high-fiber diet
  4. Spay the female
  5. Vet every 3-6 months when stable

You're doing great. Really.

Diabetes Treatment Budget for a Dog

Living with Diabetes · 6 min

Diabetes is a long-term expense, but manageable with planning. A large dog usually costs more than a small one: insulin dose and food volume scale with weight.

One-time costs (first month)

ItemRussiaEurope/US
Glucometer500-2000 ₽€20-50
Lancets (100 pcs)200-400 ₽€5-10
Initial visit1500-5000 ₽€50-150
Blood work (chemistry + T4)2000-6000 ₽€80-250
Spaying a female (if needed)5000-20,000 ₽€150-600

Monthly costs

ItemRussiaEurope/US
Insulin1500-4500 ₽€30-90
Test strips (50 pcs)800-2000 ₽€15-40
Food (canine diabetic)3000-10,000 ₽€50-150
Syringes/needles300-600 ₽€5-15

Food volume and insulin dose rise with the dog's weight, so large breeds cost more per month.

How to save (without compromising health)

Insulin:

  • Store it properly, don't extend an opened vial beyond the label
  • In Russia Caninsulin isn't registered — human NPH/glargine (U-100) are used and available at regular pharmacies
  • Don't order insulin from abroad or switch products yourself — concentration (U-40/U-100) and action profile differ

Strips:

  • A human glucometer is cheaper; in dogs it reads less low than in cats
  • Buy strips in bulk, don't test daily in a stable dog
  • A continuous sensor (FreeStyle Libre) can beat daily strips when building curves

Food:

  • Canine diabetic diets: Royal Canin Diabetic Canine, Hill's w/d, Farmina Vet Life Diabetic
  • Don't "save" by switching to cat food — it's unsuitable for a dog and raises pancreatitis risk (treatment costs more than the savings)

Vet:

  • Ask about package workups and loyalty programs
  • Telemedicine is cheaper for follow-up checks

Emergency costs

Keep a safety fund:

  • Ketoacidosis (hospital): 10,000-60,000 ₽ / €300-2500
  • Cataract surgery (optional, to preserve sight): 30,000-80,000 ₽ / €1500-4000 per eye
  • Workup for sudden deterioration: 5000-15,000 ₽ / €100-500

Tracking expenses

Use the "Expenses" section in DiaPet — built to show spending by category and the big picture.

Bottom line: diabetes is a manageable disease. With stable control, costs are predictable. And prevention (spaying, the right diet, treating infections promptly) saves on expensive complications.

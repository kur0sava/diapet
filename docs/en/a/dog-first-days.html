The First Week After Diagnosis (Dog)

Living with Diabetes · 7 min

The diagnosis is in. You're in shock, and that's normal. Thousands of dog owners have been through this and made it. This article is your plan for the first week. Take one step per day.

Day 1: Take a Deep Breath

  • Don't panic. Canine diabetes is a routine, not a death sentence
  • Write down the insulin name, dose, and injection times your vet prescribed
  • Get everything you need (list below)
  • Feed your dog as usual. Abrupt diet changes are not for today

What to Buy

Essential:

  • Insulin (as prescribed — usually Caninsulin/Vetsulin or NPH)
  • Syringes matching your insulin's concentration: U-40 for Caninsulin, U-100 for NPH/glargine. Mixing them up is dangerous!
  • Glucometer (AlphaTRAK 2/3 in "dog" mode, or a human one)
  • Test strips and lancets
  • Diabetic dog food — high fiber (not the low-carb cat kind!)
  • Honey or glucose syrup — for hypoglycemia emergencies

Nice to have:

  • Sharps disposal container
  • Kitchen scale for portions
  • A logging app (DiaPet)

An Important Question for Females: Spaying

If you have an unspayed female, be sure to discuss spaying with your vet. The hormone progesterone causes strong insulin resistance, and without spaying, stabilizing diabetes is very difficult — sometimes impossible. This is one of the key differences of canine diabetes.

Days 2-3: First Injections

The scariest part — and the most overrated. After 2-3 times, it becomes routine.

  • Ask your vet to demonstrate the technique in person
  • The insulin needle is very thin — most dogs barely feel it
  • Inject into a skin fold at the scruff or flank
  • Not sure the dose went in? Do not re-inject. Better to skip than overdose

Days 3-4: First Home Glucose Reading

  • The ear margin is a convenient spot. Warm it with your fingers
  • Don't squeeze — wait for the drop to form
  • The first few times will feel awkward. That's normal
  • Best timing: morning, before injection and food

Days 5-7: Finding Your Rhythm

By the end of the week you should have a working routine:

  1. Morning: reading → feeding → injection
  2. Evening: feeding → injection
  3. Log the results
  4. Consistent walks — the same amount of exercise every day (a sudden burst drops blood sugar)

What NOT to Do in the First Week

  • Don't switch food abruptly — transition gradually over 5-7 days
  • Don't do a full glucose curve yet — that's for a later stage
  • Don't adjust the insulin dose on your own
  • Don't give table scraps — they destabilize blood sugar

When to Call the Vet

  • Your dog refuses food for more than 12 hours
  • Glucose below 4 mmol/L — signs of hypoglycemia
  • Vomiting, lethargy, acetone breath (signs of ketoacidosis)
  • You're unsure about the dose or technique
The golden rule for week one: don't try to become an expert in 7 days. Your job is to master the basic routine. The rest comes with time.

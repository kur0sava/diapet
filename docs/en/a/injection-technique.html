Insulin Injection Technique

First — Let's Talk About Your Feelings

If your hands shake at the thought of giving an injection — that's completely normal. Almost every diabetic cat parent started with the same feeling. "I can't do this," "I'll hurt them" — sound familiar?

Here's the truth: within a week or two, this will become routine, no harder than opening a can of cat food. You can do this.

Preparation: Step by Step

1. If the insulin is refrigerated, take it out ahead of time

  • ProZinc or Caninsulin — gently roll the vial between your palms 10-20 times. Don't shake!
  • Glargine and Detemir — clear, no mixing needed
  • Let it warm in your hands for 1-2 minutes. Cold insulin can sting more

2. Draw the dose

  • Wipe the vial stopper with an alcohol swab
  • Draw air into the syringe equal to the dose, inject air into the vial
  • Flip the vial, draw the correct dose
  • Make sure there are no air bubbles
  • Double-check the dose!

3. Prepare your cat

  • Feed your cat first. Make sure they've eaten at least half their meal
  • Talk to them in a calm voice. Cats sense your nervousness

Where to Inject: The Best Spots

  • Scruff (between the shoulder blades) — the classic spot, lots of loose skin
  • Flanks (behind the ribs) — a great alternative
  • The area between the scruff and the middle of the back

Important: rotate your sites! Don't inject in the exact same spot every time — this can cause lumps, and insulin absorption will suffer.

The Injection Itself: 4 Simple Steps

  1. Tent the skin — gently pinch and pull up the skin with two fingers, creating a "tent"
  2. Insert the needle — with a quick motion into the base of the tent at a 45-degree angle
  3. Inject the insulin — smoothly press the plunger all the way down
  4. Remove the needle — wait 2-3 seconds, then withdraw. Praise your cat!

"What If I'm Not Sure the Full Dose Went In?"

  • Fur is wet at the injection site? The insulin may have leaked. But don't re-inject!
  • The needle went through both sides of the fold? It happens. Next time, make a bigger fold
  • Your cat flinched and ran away? If the plunger was at least partially pushed — don't top it up

Golden rule: when in doubt — skip. One missed dose is safer than a double dose.

Tips from Experienced Cat Parents

  • Give the injection while your cat is eating — they're distracted and often don't even notice
  • Use the thinnest needles (29-31 gauge) — they're virtually painless
  • Don't wipe the skin with alcohol — the smell scares cats and it's not necessary for subcutaneous injections
  • After the injection — a treat or cuddles. Let the injection become associated with something pleasant
  • Ask your vet to demonstrate the technique at your first appointment

You're Doing Amazing

Every injection you give is an act of love. Before long, you'll be doing it on autopilot while your kitty purrs contentedly. Trust us — that day is coming.

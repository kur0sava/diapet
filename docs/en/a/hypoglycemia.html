Hypoglycemia: How to Recognize It and What to Do

Tips · 7 min

Hypoglycemia: Low Blood Sugar

What It Is and Why It's Dangerous

Hypoglycemia is when blood glucose drops below normal (below 3.3 mmol/L or 60 mg/dL). Your cat's brain uses glucose as its primary fuel. When glucose runs too low, the brain starts to "shut down."

This can happen fast — within minutes. We know this is frightening. But a prepared pet parent means a saved cat.

Warning Signs

Early (mild hypo):

  • Unusual hunger — your cat is insistently begging for food
  • Mild trembling or shaking
  • Restlessness, meowing for no reason
  • Lethargy, reluctance to move

Moderate:

  • Staggering, wobbly walk, loss of coordination
  • "Glazed" eyes — open but unfocused
  • Weakness — can't jump up, legs seem to buckle

Severe (emergency!):

  • Seizures
  • Loss of consciousness
  • Cat doesn't respond to touch

First Aid: WHAT TO DO RIGHT NOW

Step 1: CHECK CONSCIOUSNESS

This is the most important step! Is your cat conscious and responsive? Or unconscious / having seizures?

  • Conscious → you can give sugar orally (step 2)
  • Unconscious or seizing → Do NOT put anything in their mouth! Rub only a minimal thin layer of honey on the gums (don't let it pool in the mouth — aspiration risk) and rush to the clinic immediately (they need IV dextrose 50%). If in doubt, skip this step and go straight to the clinic

Step 2: Apply something sweet to the gums (if cat is conscious)

Honey, corn syrup, or glucose solution — apply to the gums in a thin layer, don't pour into the throat!

How much depends on your cat's weight:

Cat's weightHoney / syrupHow
Under 3 kg (6.6 lb)0.25-0.5 tsp (1-2 mL)On the gums
3-5 kg (6.6-11 lb)0.5-1 tsp (2-4 mL)On the gums
5-8 kg (11-17.6 lb)1-1.5 tsp (4-6 mL)On the gums
Over 8 kg (17.6 lb)1.5-2 tsp (6-8 mL)On the gums

Step 3: Wait 5-10 minutes

Improvement usually comes within 5-10 minutes: your cat becomes more active, meows, reaches for food. If no improvement after 10 minutes → repeat the honey dose → rush to the clinic.

Step 4: Feed them if they're conscious

As soon as your cat can swallow — offer wet food immediately.

Step 5: Check glucose and call your vet

Measure glucose after 15-20 minutes and after 1 hour. Even if they seem better — notify your vet. Do not give the next insulin dose until you've consulted with them.

Three Levels of Hypoglycemia

GlucoseSignsAction
3.3-4.0 mmol/L (60-72 mg/dL)Lethargy, decreased appetiteHoney on gums + food
2.8-3.3 mmol/L (50-60 mg/dL)Staggering, disorientation, cold extremitiesHoney + food + vet within hours
Below 2.8 mmol/L (< 50 mg/dL)Seizures, loss of consciousness, comaClinic ONLY! IV dextrose

Insulin Overdose — What to Do

If you accidentally gave more insulin than prescribed:

  1. Act immediately — don't wait for symptoms
  2. Feed your cat a full meal right now
  3. If the cat is eating — honey 0.5 tsp + food
  4. Check glucose every 30 minutes for 4 hours
  5. Call your vet: report the insulin type, dose, time given, and current glucose
  6. Glucose below 4 mmol/L → honey on gums + intensive feeding
  7. Skip the next dose; resume only after consulting your vet

Cat Vomited After Eating (insulin already given)

  1. How much did they eat before vomiting? (full meal → vomited 30+ min later = most was absorbed)
  2. Offer a small portion of different, gentle food
  3. If vomiting again → don't feed, check glucose every 30-60 min
  4. Glucose below 5 mmol/L → honey on gums (protocol above)
  5. Cat hasn't eaten for 4+ hours after vomiting → vet same day

Missed Injection — Action Table

When you noticedWhat to do
Less than 2 h from scheduled timeGive the usual dose
2-4 hGive 50-75% of the dose
4-6 hGive 50% dose, next one at the scheduled time
More than 6 h or less than 2 h until nextSkip it, give the normal dose at next scheduled time
Entire cycle (12 h) missedNext scheduled dose — normal amount. Never double!

After a missed dose: check glucose before the next injection. If above 20 mmol/L — call your vet.

Why This Happens

  • Too much insulin — the number one cause
  • Cat didn't eat, but insulin was given
  • Unusual physical activity
  • Vomiting or diarrhea after eating — food wasn't absorbed, but insulin is working
  • Accidental double dose — two family members each gave an injection

How to Prevent It

  • Always feed before the injection. Didn't eat half? Don't inject
  • Never give a double dose. Not sure it went in? Skip it
  • Agree within your household on who gives the injection. Mark it in the app
  • Measure glucose before the injection if you have a glucometer

Build a "Hypo Kit"

Keep within reach:

  • Honey or corn syrup (a squeeze tube is convenient)
  • A syringe without a needle (for applying to gums)
  • Your vet's phone number and nearest 24-hour clinic
  • Wet food — a portion set aside "just in case"

You've Got This

With quick action, hypoglycemia responds very well to treatment. Honey on the gums is simple, fast, and effective. You already know what to do. A prepared pet parent is the best protection a cat can have.

Insulin Injection Technique for Dogs

Treatment · 5 min

First — about your feelings

If your hands shake at the thought of an injection — that's normal. Almost every diabetic dog owner started the same way. Within a week or two it becomes routine.

Preparation

1. Take the insulin out ahead of time

  • Caninsulin/Vetsulin, NPH are suspensions: gently roll the vial between your palms 10-15 times until uniform. Don't shake — foam and bubbles distort the dose
  • Glargine, detemir — clear, no mixing needed
  • Let it warm in your hands 1-2 minutes — cold insulin stings more

2. Draw the dose with the correct syringe

  • Caninsulin U-40 → U-40 syringe only
  • NPH/glargine U-100 → U-100 syringe only
  • Mixing them up = wrong dose = dangerous
  • Remove air bubbles, double-check the dose

3. Feed your dog

  • Make sure they ate at least half the portion. Didn't eat — don't inject, contact your vet

Where to inject

Subcutaneously, into loose skin:

  • Scruff (between the shoulder blades) — the classic, lots of loose skin
  • Side of the chest/abdomen — a good alternative
  • Along the back, to the side of the spine

Rotate sites! Repeated injections in one spot cause lumps and worsen absorption.

The injection itself

  1. Tent the skin with two fingers — you get a "tent"
  2. Insert the needle at the base of the tent: 45° for small/thin dogs, 90° for large breeds with thick subcutaneous tissue. Aim for the subcutaneous layer, not muscle and not "through and through"
  3. Inject the insulin with a smooth press of the plunger all the way
  4. Wait 2-3 seconds, withdraw the needle. Praise your dog and give a treat (within the daily allowance)

If you're unsure the dose went in

  • Wet fur at the site? Insulin may have leaked — but don't re-inject
  • Needle went through both sides of the fold? It happens. Make a bigger fold next time
  • Dog flinched? If the plunger was at least partly pressed — don't top it up

Golden rule: when in doubt — skip. One missed dose is safer than a double dose.

Large vs small dogs

  • Large breeds: thicker subcutaneous layer — 90° more often, make sure the needle doesn't reach muscle
  • Small and thin: thin skin — 45° angle, careful fold, higher "through and through" risk
  • For long-haired dogs, part the fur to see the skin

Tips

  • Inject at a calm moment, even during a meal — the dog is distracted
  • Use the thinnest needles (29-31 gauge)
  • Alcohol isn't needed for subcutaneous injections
  • Follow with a treat or affection to link the procedure to something pleasant
  • Ask your vet to demonstrate at the first appointment
Every injection is an act of care. Soon you'll be doing it on autopilot.

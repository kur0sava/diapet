The First Week After Diagnosis

The diagnosis is in. You're in shock, you're scared, and that's completely normal. Thousands of owners have been through this — and they made it. You will too.

This article is your step-by-step plan for the first week. You don't need to memorize everything at once. Take it one step at a time.

Day 1: Take a Deep Breath

  • Don't panic. Diabetes is serious, but it's not a death sentence
  • Write down the insulin name, dose, and injection times your vet prescribed
  • Get everything you need (list below)
  • Feed your cat as usual. Diet changes are not for today

What to Buy

Essential:

  • Insulin (as prescribed by your vet)
  • Insulin syringes U-40 or U-100 (depending on insulin) or pen
  • Pet glucometer (AlphaTRAK 2, or a human one works too)
  • Test strips for the glucometer
  • Lancets
  • Low-carbohydrate wet food (Hill's m/d, Purina DM, or Fancy Feast Classic)
  • Honey or sugar syrup (for hypoglycemia emergencies!)

Nice to have:

  • Sharps disposal container
  • Notebook or app for logging readings (like DiaPet)
  • Kitchen scale (for food portions)

Days 2-3: First Injections

This is the scariest part — and the most overrated. After 2-3 times, it becomes routine.

  • Ask your vet to demonstrate the injection technique in person
  • The insulin needle is so thin that most cats don't even feel it
  • Inject into a skin fold on the scruff or side
  • If you're not sure you gave the full dose — do NOT inject again. It's better to miss than to overdose

Days 3-4: First Home Glucose Reading

  • The ear vein is the easiest spot. Warm the ear with your fingers before pricking
  • Don't squeeze the ear — wait for the drop to form on its own
  • The first few times will feel awkward. That's normal
  • Best timing: morning, before injection and food

Days 5-7: Finding Your Rhythm

By the end of the week, you should have a working routine:

  1. Morning: glucose check → feeding → injection
  2. Evening: feeding → injection
  3. Log results in your diary

What NOT to Do in the First Week

  • Don't switch food abruptly — transition gradually over 5-7 days
  • Don't try to do a full glucose curve — that's for experienced owners
  • Don't read horror stories on forums — every cat is different
  • Don't adjust the insulin dose on your own

When to Call the Vet

  • Your cat refuses food for more than 12 hours
  • Glucose below 4 mmol/L (72 mg/dL) — signs of hypoglycemia
  • Vomiting, lethargy, acetone breath (signs of ketoacidosis)
  • You're unsure about the dose or technique
The golden rule for week one: don't try to become an expert in 7 days. Your job is to master the basic routine. Everything else will come with time.

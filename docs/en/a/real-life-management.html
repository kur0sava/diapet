Living with a Diabetic Cat: When Things Don't Go as Planned

Textbooks describe an ideal world: feeding on the dot, injections to the minute, glucose curves every week. Real life is different. And that's okay.

Late with the Injection

Long-acting insulin (Lantus, Levemir) forgives 1-2 hour deviations. If you're late:

  • Up to 2 hours — inject as normal
  • 2-4 hours — inject the usual dose, shift the next injection
  • More than 6 hours — skip this dose, give the next one on schedule
  • Never give a double dose!

Cat Didn't Finish Eating

This is common, especially with picky cats:

  • If she ate more than half — give the full dose
  • If she ate less than half — give half the dose
  • If she didn't eat at all — do NOT inject, call the vet

Travel / Vacation

Option 1: Cat stays home, someone comes to help

  • Train the person to give injections (2-3 practice sessions)
  • Leave written instructions: dose, timing, food, emergency contacts
  • An insulin pen is easier for beginners than a syringe
  • Video call on the first day to supervise

Option 2: Cat at a boarding facility

  • Warn them in advance — not all facilities accept diabetic cats
  • Leave insulin, syringes, food, and instructions
  • Ask for daily photos/videos

Option 3: Cat travels with you

  • Insulin in a cooler bag (don't freeze it!)
  • Travel stress may raise blood sugar — this is temporary
  • Bring honey for hypoglycemia emergencies

Multiple Cats at Home

  • The diabetic must eat separately from other cats
  • Low-carb food is safe for healthy cats — you can switch everyone
  • Feed the diabetic first, then the others
  • Remove leftover food after 30 minutes

Irregular Work Schedule

Working shifts? Injections can be adapted:

  • Interval between injections: 10-14 hours (ideal is 12)
  • If you work nights: morning injection before bed, evening injection after waking up
  • The key is consistency: same time every day is better than chaos

Cat Vomited After Eating

  • If less than 15 minutes after eating and injection — check sugar in 2 hours
  • If sugar is dropping — offer a small amount of food
  • If vomiting continues — call the vet

Ran Out of Insulin / Syringes

  • Always keep a 2-week supply
  • Order new insulin in advance — some pharmacies need to order it
  • If insulin runs out: one day without insulin isn't catastrophic for a stable cat, but call your vet
The main takeaway: diabetes is a marathon, not a sprint. You don't need to be perfect every day. You need to be good enough most of the time.
